2  Methods

This analysis primarily uses data from the National Bridge Inventory (Download NBI ASCII Files - National Bridge Inventory - Bridge Inspection - Safety Inspection - Bridges & Structures - Federal Highway Administration, n.d.) and Federal Highway Administration traffic data (Highway Statistics 2023 - Policy Federal Highway Administration, n.d.). Additional information from Nebraska Department of Transportation reports (Bridge - NDOT, n.d.) is used to provide context for maintenance and repair decisions.

Only bridges located in Douglas County, Nebraska, which includes Omaha, were included in the analysis. Bridges with missing structural condition ratings were excluded to ensure consistent results.

2.1 Data

2.1.1 National Bridge Inventory

The data comes from the Federal Highway Administration’s National Bridge Inventory (Download NBI ASCII Files - National Bridge Inventory - Bridge Inspection - Safety Inspection - Bridges & Structures - Federal Highway Administration, n.d.). This dataset provides information about bridge age, condition ratings, location, and traffic levels. The main variables used from this dataset are shown in Table 2.1.

These variables help show the condition of each bridge. Lower condition ratings indicate a greater chance of structural problems. Bridge age is also an important factor because older bridges are more likely to show wear over time.

There is a possible issue with missing data in the dataset. Some bridges may not have complete ratings for every condition measure. If data is missing, those bridges will either be removed from the analysis or reviewed carefully so the results are not misleading.

Nebraska Department of Transportation

These state bridge reports are from NDOT (Bridge - NDOT, n.d.). This source adds more local information about repairs and maintenance plans, which can help when looking at short term risk. The main variables used from this source are shown in Table 2.2.

This source is useful because a bridge with planned repairs or available funding may have lower short term risk than a bridge with no repair plans. It also helps add more local context that may not be fully shown in the national bridge inventory data.

2.1.2 Traffic Data

This is traffic data that comes from FHWA Highway Statistics. (Highway Statistics 2023 - Policy Federal Highway Administration, n.d.). This source is mainly used to help estimate the consequences of a bridge collapse instead of the probability of one actually happening.The main variables used from this source are shown in Table 2.3.

2.1.2.1 Important Variables

Table 2.3 helps show how serious the consequences would be if a bridge collapsed. A bridge that carries a high amount of traffic, especially truck traffic, would more likely cause more problems.

2.1.3 Is this enough data?

I do think it is enough data. Each source provides me with different things that could help assess and prevent the bridge collapse risk in Omaha over the next five years. The NBI data provides the probability of a bridge collapsing. The NDOT data helps with local repair information, and the traffic data measure the impact. I think they work together very well.

2.2 Selection of Relevant Variables

The variables used in this analysis are shown in Table 2.1, Table 2.2, and Table 2.3. The main variables include bridge age, deck rating, superstructure rating, substructure rating, lowest condition rating, bridge condition, and average daily traffic.

Bridge age was calculated using the year built. Bridge condition was measured using deck, superstructure, and substructure ratings. A bridge was classified as being in poorer condition if its lowest condition rating was 4 or below.

2.3 Evaluating Risk

To estimate the probability of failure, bridges with lower condition ratings were treated as higher risk. To estimate the consequences of failure, average daily traffic was used as the measure of impact, with higher traffic indicating greater potential disruption.

Overall bridge risk was estimated using the formula:

Risk = Probability of Failure × Consequences of Failure

This approach was used to identify bridges that may require additional monitoring or maintenance, as discussed in Chapter 3.
