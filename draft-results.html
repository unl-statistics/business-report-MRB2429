Methods

This analysis primarily uses data from the National Bridge Inventory (“Download NBI ASCII Files - National Bridge Inventory - Bridge Inspection - Safety Inspection - Bridges & Structures - Federal Highway Administration” n.d.) and Federal Highway Administration traffic data (“Highway Statistics 2023 - Policy Federal Highway Administration” n.d.). Additional information from Nebraska Department of Transportation reports (“Bridge - NDOT” n.d.) is used to provide context for maintenance and repair decisions.

Only bridges located in Douglas County, Nebraska, which includes Omaha, were included in the analysis. Bridges with missing structural condition ratings were excluded to ensure consistent results.

The variables used in this analysis are shown in ?@tbl-nbi, ?@tbl-ndot, and ?@tbl-traffic. The main variables include bridge age, deck rating, superstructure rating, substructure rating, lowest condition rating, bridge condition, and average daily traffic.

Bridge age was calculated using the year built. Bridge condition was measured using deck, superstructure, and substructure ratings. A bridge was classified as being in poorer condition if its lowest condition rating was 4 or below.

To estimate the probability of failure, bridges with lower condition ratings were treated as higher risk. To estimate the consequences of failure, average daily traffic was used as the measure of impact, with higher traffic indicating greater potential disruption.

Overall bridge risk was estimated using the formula:

Risk = Probability of Failure × Consequences of Failure

This approach was used to identify bridges that may require additional monitoring or maintenance.

Results

Data Characteristics

The data set includes 511 bridges in the Omaha (Douglas County) area. The average bridge age is about 45 years, showing a mix of older and newer structures. Most bridges have condition ratings that fall within fair to good categories.

Out of the 511 bridges analyzed, 16 bridges have a condition rating of 4 or below, indicating poorer condition. This represents about 3% of all bridges, suggesting that most bridges are not currently in poor condition.

Traffic levels vary across bridges. Some carry only a small number of vehicles per day, while others carry over 100,000 vehicles per day, especially on major highways.

Primary Analysis: Probability of Failure

The analysis shows that bridges with lower condition ratings have a higher risk of failure. Bridges with a lowest condition rating of 4 or below were identified as higher risk bridges.

The results also show that older bridges tend to have lower condition ratings. This suggests that bridge condition declines over time due to wear and usage. While most bridges are still in acceptable condition, there is a small group of bridges with lower ratings that may be at higher risk.

Secondary Analysis: Consequences of Failure

Traffic data shows that bridges with higher daily traffic would cause the largest disruptions if they failed. Bridges that carry very high traffic volumes, especially on major roads like I-80, would have the greatest impact on transportation and public safety.

Information from NDOT (“Bridge - NDOT” n.d.) provides context on maintenance and planned repairs, which may help reduce short-term risk. However, bridges that have both high traffic and low condition ratings represent the highest overall risk.

These higher risk bridges are typically located along major transportation routes in Omaha. Since these routes are heavily used, a failure in these areas would cause major traffic disruptions and safety concerns.

Calculation of Bridge Risk

Overall bridge risk combines the probability of failure and the consequences of failure:

Risk = Probability of Failure × Consequence of Failure

As shown in Figure 1, bridge condition is used to estimate failure risk, and traffic data is used to estimate the impact of a failure.

Key Findings

  • Most bridges in Omaha are in fair to good condition
  • Only 16 out of 511 bridges (~3%) are in poorer condition and may need monitoring or repair
  • Older bridges tend to have lower condition ratings, showing gradual deterioration
  • Bridges with high traffic volumes would cause the biggest disruptions if they failed
  • Bridges with both low condition ratings and high traffic represent the highest risk
Important

Conclusion

Overall, widespread bridge failure in Omaha is unlikely. However, targeted monitoring and maintenance of the small number of lower-rated bridges is recommended over the next five years to reduce overall risk.
